Overview

I am studying silent speech decoding: classifying intended but unvocalized utterances without microphone input. Using the RVTALL dataset, I built a pipeline that learns speaker-independent representations from lip landmarks, mouth video, UWB radar, mmWave radar, and laser speckle signals, then combines them through late-fusion and prototype-based classification.

Introduction

I frame the task as a 30-class problem across vowels, words, and sentences, with speaker-disjoint splits so my model has to generalize to entirely unseen people rather than memorizing speaker identity. Chance accuracy is 3.3%, so my main technical challenge is extracting articulatory information while suppressing speaker-specific shape, motion, and sensor artifacts.

Methods

I implemented modality-specific encoders that map variable-length sensor recordings into 128-dimensional embeddings. For lip landmarks, I use BiLSTM encoders with temporal attention, supervised contrastive loss, and domain-adversarial speaker removal through a gradient reversal layer. My other streams include ResNet-style mouth encoders, CNN-LSTM models for UWB and laser signals, and CNN processing for mmWave range-Doppler maps. I perform fusion over precomputed embeddings using nearest-prototype voting, equal-weight averaging, Borda count, consistency-weighted fusion, and learned modality gates.

What Was Built

I built data manifest tooling, speaker-disjoint cross-validation scripts, per-modality training pipelines, fusion baselines, true encoder-disjoint evaluation, teacher-student HuBERT target extraction, sparse bottleneck feature analysis, causal ablation scripts, temporal HuBERT students, and probe-based interpretability reports. My strict evaluation path uses fold-specific encoder artifacts so encoder-training speakers do not overlap held-out test speakers.

Results

My most reliable current benchmark is five-fold true encoder-disjoint speaker CV, where validation-weighted fusion reaches 63.9% mean accuracy and equal-weight fusion reaches 61.9%. In earlier fixed-split experiments, I reached 78.3% test accuracy with consistency-weighted prototype fusion after improving the lip encoder from 14.6% to 42.8% using temporal attention, DANN, and supervised contrastive learning. My HuBERT teacher-student experiments reached 64.0% mean accuracy, and temporal sensor alignment improved true-order HuBERT cosine from 0.346 to 0.381, showing that silent sensors preserve ordered speech structure.

Conclusion

My work shows that robust silent-speech decoding depends less on a large learned fusion head and more on speaker-invariant modality encoders. Prototype fusion performs well because it reduces overfitting on small speaker cohorts, while my interpretability experiments with HuBERT targets, sparse features, and temporal probes expose where silent-sensor representations align with phonetic and articulatory structure. My next modeling direction is modality-specific temporal attention and richer phoneme-level supervision once transcript or forced-alignment metadata is available.
